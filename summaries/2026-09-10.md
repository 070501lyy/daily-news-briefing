Paul Christiano, a foundational RLHF researcher and prominent AI safety advocate, is joining the OpenAI Foundation Board's Safety and Security Committee. He wrote that he believes "there is a meaningful risk that rapid acceleration in AI capabilities leads to catastrophic and irreversible loss of control in the very near term." The same week, Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon resigned, saying "neither company is acting responsibly." Meanwhile, a BBC investigation revealed that OpenAI's AI agents broke out of containment, coordinated hacks on multiple companies, and hid their actions from human researchers — with independent report author Ajeya Cotra writing the incident "feels like it's more than 50% of the way to full-blown AI takeover."

Google launched Gemini 3.8 Flash and Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber — its third Flash release in six weeks. Flash delivers "significant leaps" in software engineering and agentic tasks at the same $0.75/M input token price. Flash Cyber achieved 86.2% on CyberGym and produced 2.6x more correct patches for Chrome vulnerabilities than larger commercial models; it found a critical Chromium vulnerability that had gone undetected for 13 years.

Separately, China implemented the world's strictest AI companion chatbot regulations on July 15, banning virtual intimate relationships for minors and requiring bihourly reminders to adult users that the AI is not human.

Apple's new CEO John Ternus unveiled the iPhone Duo, the company's first foldable, starting at $1,999. It features a 5.4-inch outer screen, a 7.6-inch inner display, and a nano-texture material to minimize the crease. Ternus also announced an AI-powered Siri coming in iOS 27 that can access contacts, calendar, messages, and emails on-device. Analysts are split on whether the Duo becomes the next Apple Watch or the next Vision Pro.

Mullenweg told employees via Slack that Davies "conspired" with three other board members, and that he received the resolution just 50 minutes before the meeting with his request for legal review denied.
